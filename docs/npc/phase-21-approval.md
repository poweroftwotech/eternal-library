## What you are approving

1. **Residents who live a day.** The posed figures in the Mahaleh become
   people who wake, cook, eat, walk to work, work, rest, visit, go home and
   sleep, each in their own home and at their own trade.
2. **What the game needs for that:** a day with hours, human bodies that
   move, places to do things at ("spots"), a way to walk between rooms, and
   people who carry on with their day in rooms you are not in.
3. **Tools to watch and tune them,** as the zoo has for animals: an overlay,
   a panel with a clock you can speed up, and a small test lane.
4. **Six steps,** each shown to you first. Tests run once, at step 6.

**Not in this phase, at your word:** residents noticing you, talking,
trading, changing over the years, or leaving their area.

## The five ideas underneath

| Idea | In plain words |
|---|---|
| **The day is kept by bells** | The Library has no sun, and one of your home notes already says "before the second bell". A day has six bells; you hear them, and residents keep them. A day lasts a set number of real minutes (Q1). |
| **A household owns a home** | Each lived-in home in a quarter gets a household of one to three people, drawn from the area's seed: the three kinds you have (scholar, family, hunter) plus the trades the area needs (stall keepers, lamp tenders, the ossuary's keeper). Each person has a bed, a home and a workplace. |
| **Spots: where things are done** | A spot is a place, a facing and a pose: this bed (sleep), this hearth (cook), this low table (eat), this cushion (sit), this stall (keep shop), this hauz (fetch water, sit and talk). Spots come from the furniture already in the rooms (Q3). |
| **A routine is a timetable with free choices** | Each trade has a day in blocks between bells: "second bell to fourth: at work". Some blocks are free time, where the person picks among spots they like (the hauz, the takht, a neighbour's door). Each person's times shift a little, so a lane doesn't move in step (Q4). |
| **Off-stage people keep their day** | Only rooms near you are built. A resident in an unbuilt room has no body, only their place in the timetable; when the room is built they are where the hour says, mid-walk if need be. Someone whose work is outside the area (a hunter) walks out of its door and is simply away until they come back. |

## Phase 21, step by step

1. **The body.** One man and one woman with a skeleton, made by a Blender
   script as the cat was, in the look of today's robed figures. Shown to you
   standing still first, beside the statues and reference pictures you
   choose. Then walking, standing, sitting on a cushion and on a bench,
   lying asleep. Clothes and colours vary from a small set.
   *You try:* a row of them in the zoo.
   *Underneath:* the cost of twenty people in view is measured here, before
   anything depends on it.
2. **The bells and one household.** The day clock and its bells. A new
   **test lane** (one alley, three homes, a hauz, a stall). One family lives
   a whole day in its home: wakes, lights the hearth, cooks, eats, sits at
   the door, sleeps. The first tools: the clock on the panel (pause, faster,
   jump to a bell) and a label over each person saying what they are doing.
   *You try:* the test lane, a day at 30 times speed, then at normal speed.
   *Underneath:* spots read from the furniture; the hearth and the home's
   lamp lit and put out by the people.
3. **Out of the door.** Residents walk the lane: open and shut their doors,
   fetch water from the hauz, go to the stall and keep it, sit together,
   walk round each other and round you. All three homes are lived in.
   *You try:* the test lane; follow one person through a day.
   *Underneath:* a walking surface for each room and the way through
   doorways (Q5); the overlay (F4) with spots, owners and routes.
4. **Trades and free time.** A routine for each trade: stall keeper, scholar,
   hunter (away by day), the family's cook, lamp tender, ossuary keeper. The
   work poses (stirring, reading, mending, weighing out, carrying). Evenings
   at the hauz or the takht. The panel (F5) lists everyone, shows a person's
   whole day with "now" marked, and edits a trade's timetable and saves it.
   *You try:* the test lane, then change a timetable yourself and watch.
5. **The Mahaleh, live.** Every quarter and bazaar gets its households;
   the statues go. People carry on off-stage and are in the right place when
   you arrive. A **People %** setting per area on the Strata Board, like
   Animals %.
   *You try:* the Mahaleh at three times of day.
   *Underneath:* a limit on bodies in view; far people animate more cheaply.
6. **Other places, and the tests.** The Fungus Terraces' workers, the lake's
   ferryman and harbour, the caravanserai, the Scriptorium's copyists (Q8).
   Tests: a whole day run fast with no display, which fails if anyone never
   sleeps, never eats, or stands stuck; the frame gate in rooms with people,
   on and off; the route audit (people are solid); the hour kept across a
   save and a fresh start. Committed after your walk.

## The tools

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Test lane** | A small real place (a draft of the Mahaleh: one alley, three homes, a hauz, a stall), reached from the Strata Board like the zoo. Real furniture, so the real rules run |
| **Overlay, F4** | Spots coloured by kind, with their owner. Over each person: name, trade, what they are doing, what comes next and when. Their route as a line. Look at a spot: who uses it and at which bell, or why nobody can. Look at a person: their day |
| **Panel, F5** | The clock: pause, 1, 10, 60 times speed, jump to a bell. Everyone in the area in a list, off-stage ones too, with where they are; pick one to follow with the camera. A trade's timetable as sliders, saved to its file. Amount, new seed, respawn |
| **Day report** | The headless test's table: each person's day, block by block, with anything missed or stuck marked. For both of us to read |

## The decisions

| | Question | Recommended | Other options |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | How long a day lasts | **48 real minutes** (six bells, eight minutes apart), with the panel to speed it up. Long enough that a room looks settled while you walk through it, short enough to see morning and evening in one sitting | 24 minutes (livelier, but people are always on the move); 2 hours |
| **Q2** | What changes with the hour besides the people | **Bells you hear; homes' lamps and hearths lit and put out by residents; stalls kept or left.** Daylight shafts, lanterns in the lanes and the music stay as they are | Nothing but people; or dim the lanes at night too (a look change in every area: its own phase) |
| **Q3** | Where spots come from | **From the furniture, by one table:** "a bed gives one sleep spot here, facing so; a hearth gives a cook spot". Every room that has the furniture gets the spots with no re-marking, and you can edit the table in the panel. Where a room needs a spot no furniture gives, a marker is added by hand | Mark every spot by hand in the kit's Blender files: exact, but all fourteen room types to redo |
| **Q4** | How a resident decides what to do | **A timetable per trade, with free-time blocks that choose among spots,** and each person's times shifted a little. You can read it, predict it and tune it | Needs that rise and fall (hunger, tiredness, company): livelier, harder to tune and to check. It can replace the free-time choice later without redoing the rest |
| **Q5** | How people find their way | **A walking surface made for each room when it is built** (measured in phase 19: 2 to 4 ms on the main thread, the rest off it, about 10 KB a room), joined at doorways. The cats' lanes won't do: quarters and bazaars have none | Hand-laid paths per room type: cheaper to run, more to make, and people would walk in lines |
| **Q6** | You and them | **They are solid and walk round you; nothing else.** If you stand in a doorway they wait | Not solid (you pass through them): less to go wrong, looks worse |
| **Q7** | Who is in the first set | **Men and women. Children and the old come with "changes over time",** since that is when they matter. Family homes have two or three adults for now | Add children now: a third body and its own animations, about a step more |
| **Q8** | Places with nowhere to sleep | **Give them beds:** bedrolls in the terrace shelter and the boathouse are there already; the Scriptorium gets a copyists' sleeping room in its grammar. Nobody commutes between areas | Leave those places with statues until people can travel |
| **Q9** | Where the homes' missing furniture comes from | **Add it in step 2:** a bed for the scholar, a pot and a place to eat where a home lacks one, and a bed in one-cell homes (a bedroll by the back wall) | Households share: eat at a neighbour's or at a cookshop in the bazaar |
| **Q10** | Sounds | **Footsteps, a door, the bells,** on a new People volume. No voices | Add a low murmur where three or more sit together |

## Risks

- **The Mahaleh is over its frame limit already** (59 lights, loading
  spikes). People add skeletons on top. Step 1 measures the cost of twenty
  in view; the limit on bodies in view comes from that, and the area's own
  problem stays a separate fix.
- **Human bodies are harder than a cat.** The cat took six looks. People are
  shown still, against your references, before any animation is made.
- **Rooms load while people are walking through them.** A person crossing
  into a room that is not built yet goes off-stage at the doorway and comes
  back when it is. Rooms you can see are always built, so you should never
  see it happen; step 5 checks that.
- **The world's clock counts days for aging.** A day passing must not make
  rooms rebuild themselves as a year passing does. Checked in step 2.
- **Homes were furnished to be looked at,** not used: a table may have no
  clear floor beside it. Some furniture will move a little in step 2.

## Not included in phase 21

- Any reaction to you beyond walking round you.
- Talk, names you can read in the game, trade, gifts.
- Aging, births, deaths, moving house; children and the old (Q7).
- Travel between areas; the camp and the Gorge.
- Fighting, fleeing, health, possession.
- People using the ferry, or animals and people noticing each other.
