Costume Atlas

What the Library's people wear. The cut comes from four ages of ancient Iran: the Medes, the Achaemenids, the Parthians and the Sasanians. What it became is decided by where these people live: under stone, with no sun and no wind, in damp and cold, by lamplight.

These are sketches to agree a direction, not model sheets. Twelve figures, six men and six women, one for each kind of life the game has today. Nothing here is on the models yet.

What the old dress gives us

Each age left a clear silhouette. The sketches borrow whole pieces from them, and say which.

What living underground changes

Take away the sun, the wind and the dust, and add damp, cold stone and the dark. Ten rules follow. The ones marked in green are about light.

  • The head is uncovered by default.There is no sun to hide from. Hair is dressed and shown: curls, braids, fillets, topknots. A hat says what you do; a hood says you work where water drips.
  • Felt and wax, not linen.The enemy is damp and cold. Thick felt, quilting and waxed cloth; layers that come off by a hearth. The shepherd's stiff felt cloak becomes the ferryman's.
  • Glow thread.Thread steeped in an area's glowing fungus. It edges hems and cuffs and fills the pearls of a roundel. Each area has its own colour, so in the dark you can tell where a stranger is from. It fades, and is steeped again.
  • Discs that catch the lamp.The Achaemenids' sewn-on gold becomes polished brass discs on the chest and along edges. They light nothing; they make you seen when a lamp passes.
  • The lantern globe.The kings' silk-bound globe becomes a small lantern of oiled silk worn on the cap by those who tend the lamps. It means: this one carries fire.
  • The mouth cloth has two jobs.Lamp tenders wear it to keep their breath off the flame, as priests did. Fungus workers wear it against spores.
  • Colour runs backwards.Fungus and lichen give violets, rusts and greys cheaply, so purple is the common colour. Madder red and saffron need plants grown under a light shaft, so warm bright cloth is wealth.
  • Paper is a cloth.A library has endless ruined pages. Copyists quilt them into jackets for the cold stacks; a scholar's head ribbons are bookmarks. Only pages past reading are used.
  • Feet know the floor.Soft felt soles where people read. Wooden pattens and waxed leggings where the floor is wet.
  • The belt carries the day.The Sasanian belt with its hanging straps holds a lamp, a striker, a cup and keys. Hunters hang a shuttered lamp and wear no glow at all.

The same twelve with the lamps out

What glow thread, discs and the lantern globe would give a dark lane: people as lines and dots of light. The hunter and the ferryman vanish, by choice.

On the models this is a glow painted into the cloth. It casts no light and costs no lamps from an area's limit.
