The Gorge, calmer and lived-in

Your four notes come from one cause: the gorge is built a 4 m bay at a time, and nothing makes the bays into buildings. This board proposes a direction that composes it as a Persian hill town around a few monuments, and lays out the rules, the door and window kit, the access logic, the waterfalls and the budget it needs. The pictures are 2D mock-ups drawn for discussion, not game renders.

Built and carved, one place

In Iran, people lived in the mountain as often as on it: Kandovan's cones, Meymand's cave houses, the rock-cut iwans of Taq-e Bostan, the cliff tombs of Naqsh-e Rostam. The gorge works the same way. Every bank mixes four ways of meeting the rock, so you never see a wall stuck onto a cliff.

  • Rock, dressed rock, stone, brick, plasterMaterials change in that order as you go from cliff to building. A built wall stands on a carved footing, never straight on raw rock.
  • Carved openings look carvedThey're deeper and softer-edged than built ones, set in a smoothed panel. Some are lit: rooms behind the houses, inside the cliff.
  • Paths between terracesWhere a built terrace ends, a rock-cut path with carved doors carries the street on, so every level is one walk.
  • One water systemA spring in a carved iwan feeds a garden pool, then a channel, a cascade to the terrace below, and finally a great fall.

Light decides what grows

The gorge is underground, so green plants only grow where there's light for them. Each garden is one of three kinds, depending on where it sits. They all keep the same Persian layout: water at the centre, beds in four parts, rows along the paths.

  • Under the lightWhere the unexplained light reaches: shafts through cracks in the roof, and the far end round the sanctum. Cypress, pomegranate, flowers, grass. Few and precious; people gather there.
  • Lit by a stoneLumen stones: glowing gems on brass posts, backed by mirror mosaic (after Persian aineh-kari) to throw more light. Green beds sit inside their circle of warm-white light. Only the rich have them: madrasas, iwan gardens, a few courtyards.
  • In the darkEverywhere else: giant glowing mushrooms, jelly fungi with hanging strands, and glow moss on the rock, planted in the same rows and beds. The teal echoes the dome tiles. The game already has mushrooms to forage, so these could be farms.
  • Where the light endsEvery lit garden fades into fungi at its edge, as in render 2. That boundary is the most interesting place to look.
  • In the gameFungi are emissive materials with a few shadowless teal lights per cluster. Lumen stones are shadowless lights. Shafts are a fog volume and one light each. Pools use the Reading Garden's water shader.
  • Where gardens goOn the widest yards, in front of monuments and carved iwans, and on a few roofs. About one terrace run in four.
  • Small ones tooPots on sunlit roofs, a ring of mushrooms by a dark door, moss in the joints of a stair. That's what makes it lived-in from far away.

Facade rules

  • A building, not a bayEach building picks a type (house, madrasa front, caravanserai, tower, shrine) and repeats one opening along its front.
  • Base, body, crownStone base, darker and wet. Plain body. Crown of banai brick, a thin tile frieze and a parapet.
  • Tile in four placesPortal frame and spandrels, crown frieze, domes, minaret bands. Never a whole wall. At most about 15% of a front.
  • Quiet beats loudAcross a view, about 85% plain brick and plaster; one landmark per view.
  • Light follows peopleLamps at doors, stairs and portals, in clusters, with dark stretches between. Lit windows mean someone lives there.
  • Wear has a reasonSoot above lamps, water streaks under spouts, patched plaster at the base, moss where the mist reaches.

Access you can believe

Waterfalls with weight

Where the water comes fromFrom channel mouths in retaining walls and aqueduct spillways, three or four of them, placed as landmarks. No water out of mid-air, and no fall in every arch.
How it's drawnA curved ribbon (about 200 triangles) with two layers: an opaque core whose streaks speed up as they fall, and wider veils that fade at the edges. The lower third breaks up. A dozen mist cards and a foam ring sit at the landing.
What it costsAlmost nothing in triangles. Transparency overdraw is the cost, kept down by narrow veils and mist cards that fade near the camera. Spray particles only within reach of the Overlook. Sound gives it the power the picture can't.

Built to fit the budget

Your fly walks showed 5.9 to 7.5 million triangles against a 3 million limit, and 670 to 1,095 draw calls of 1,500. A calmer gorge is also a cheaper one, because buildings merge into single meshes where bays can't.
