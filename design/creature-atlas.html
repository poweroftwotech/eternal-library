Library Creature Atlas

Twenty animals for the first stratum: six of the water, eight of the ground and six of the air. Seventeen are real animals of Iran or of every old town; three are from Persian legend and are rare. All of them only live here for now. Each plate also says what the animal is for later (defending itself, attacking, being fished, hunted or possessed), so nothing has to be redesigned when those arrive.

An animal is not tied to an area, but each area has a few it is known by. The plan for building them is in docs/npc/phase-19-approval.md and docs/npc/phase-20-approval.md; what was built and changed is logged in docs/npc/progress/.

In the game so far

Twelve of the twenty are in the game (phase 19 and phase 20 steps 1 to 3). What holds for all of them, beyond the plan:

  • Numbers by world seedEach place rolls a level for each animal from the world seed: abundant, common, scarce or very scarce (1.5×, 1×, ½ and ⅕ of the usual numbers). An animal an area is known by is never below common. The levels are kept in the save.
  • Placed by what a room hasAnimals go where their conditions are met (water of the right depth, flames, dark high roofs, ledges, worn lanes, wall feet), not by room type. The Strata Board has an Animals % per area and an Animals box per room.
  • SoundAnimals make their own sounds: splashes, the bats' chitter, the cat's meow and purr, the rats' squeak, the nightingale's song, the pigeons' coo and wing clap.
  • Still no lights, no collisionGlowing animals glow by themselves and light nothing. Animals don't block you, so the route tests are unchanged by them.
  • The zooA test place with a pen for each habitat (--room=zoo, or Walk the zoo on the board). F4 shows each animal and what it is doing; F5 opens a tuning panel.
  • Turn them off--no-animals starts the game without any, for comparing frame times.

How animals fit a world with no sky

The rooms get their character from light, colour, motion, one landmark and life. The animals follow the same rules, and add four of their own.

  • They live where something feeds themLight shafts, lamps, water, fungus, stores of food, old paper. No animal stands in an empty corridor.
  • They tell you about the roomGeckos mean a lamp is tended. A nightingale means a garden is kept. Rats and bats mean nobody comes here any more.
  • They never add a lightGlowing animals glow by themselves and light nothing. Every area is already at its limit of lamps.
  • Natural glow shines aloneGlowworms keep a bay away from any flame or glowing fungus, as the fungi do, and take the area's fungus colour.
  • They notice youFish scatter, the cat looks up, bats leave when a light comes near. Nothing harms you yet: spooky, not dangerous.
  • Legend is rare and farThe three mythic animals are seen seldom, at a distance, one at a time. You should wonder whether you saw it.
  • Every body is a possible hostThe design foundation makes almost any creature possessable. Each plate notes what that body would be good and bad at.
  • They eat each otherMoths feed geckos and bats, rats feed cats, fish feed otters. Later, hunting one changes the numbers of another.

Kept in reserve

Good fits that didn't make the twenty. Any of them can be swapped in.

  • Hedgehog (juje-tighi)Honoured in Zoroastrian writing as a killer of ants and snakes. A gardener's friend in the Garden Court and the terraces.
  • Rooster and hens (khorus)The bird that calls the dawn and drives off the dark. Quarter courts and caravanserai stables; eggs later.
  • Hoopoe (hodhod)The guide of the birds in Attar's Conference of the Birds. An animal that leads you somewhere.
  • Freshwater crab (kharchang)Common in Iran's streams and qanats. Walks the water steps and the wading shelves.
  • Marsh frog (ghurbaghe)A voice for the rills and the cistern once the game has sound. In the old story, a frog is what the Kar fish guards against.
  • Tortoise (lak-posht)Slow keeper of the Garden Court's beds.

Held for the later monster and legend tier, because they are not passive animals: the Simurgh, the Karkadann, the Mardkhor (the manticore, a Persian word), the div, the pari and the azhdaha. The Camrus stays the companion bird of the design foundation and is not in this atlas.

About this atlas

There are no pictures here: a small animal can't be mocked honestly with the room atlas's flat-polygon painter. Each plate gives the size against you (1.75 m), the three main colours and what the animal does. The plate text is the plan as approved; the "In the game" box under it says what was built and how it differs. The animals in the game were each shown to you in the game first; the zoo is the quickest place to see them.
